## 📚 Vocabulary to Remember

- **preheat** — to heat a pan or oven to the required temperature before cooking
  - *Always preheat a stainless steel pan for two to three minutes before adding oil.*
- **shimmer** — a subtle rippling movement in oil that signals it has reached the right cooking temperature
  - *When the oil begins to shimmer, you know it's ready for the vegetables.*
- **sear** — to cook the surface of food quickly at high heat to create a browned, flavourful crust
  - *Sear the steak for two minutes on each side to lock in the juices.*
- **deglaze** — to add liquid to a hot pan and stir to dissolve the browned bits on the bottom, creating a sauce
  - *After the chicken was done, she deglazed the pan with white wine to make a simple sauce.*
- **residue** — small amounts of food or grease left on a surface after cooking
  - *Any residue left in the pan is packed with flavour and perfect for making a sauce.*
- **release** — the moment when food naturally comes away from the pan surface without being forced
  - *Don't rush it — the steak will release on its own when it is properly seared.*
- **heat retention** — the ability of a material to hold heat and stay at a stable temperature during cooking
  - *Stainless steel pans are valued for their excellent heat retention.*
- **non-stick** — describes a cooking surface coated so that food does not cling to it
  - *Many beginners start with a non-stick pan before moving on to stainless steel.*

## 🔧 Say It Naturally

1. ❌ *First make hot the pan, after that add oil.*
   ✅ **Heat the pan first, then add your oil.**
   💡 English uses 'heat' as a direct verb; 'make hot' is not standard, and 'after that' needs a full clause.
2. ❌ *Don't force it — let the food to release alone.*
   ✅ **Don't force it — let the food release on its own.**
   💡 After 'let,' English uses the base verb without 'to,' and 'on its own' is the natural phrasing.
3. ❌ *You will know the pan is ready when the oil begins to make shine.*
   ✅ **You'll know the pan is ready when the oil starts to shimmer.**
   💡 'Shimmer' is one precise verb for this visual cue; 'make shine' is not a standard English phrase.
4. ❌ *Any residue left in the pan has many flavour inside.*
   ✅ **Any residue left in the pan is packed with flavour.**
   💡 'Packed with' is the natural idiom for 'full of'; 'has many flavour' is both ungrammatical and unnatural.
5. ❌ *Cook strongly the meat on high heat for some minutes on each side.*
   ✅ **Sear the meat on high heat for a couple of minutes on each side.**
   💡 'Sear' is the precise cooking term; 'cook strongly' is not a standard phrase, and the adverb position is also wrong.
6. ❌ *If food is making stick, maybe the pan isn't enough hot.*
   ✅ **If food is sticking, the pan probably isn't hot enough yet.**
   💡 'Stick' is used intransitively here, and in English 'enough' follows the adjective it modifies — 'hot enough,' not 'enough hot.'
7. ❌ *Once you understand how to do, stainless steel is very rewarding to cook with.*
   ✅ **Once you get the hang of it, stainless steel is incredibly rewarding to cook with.**
   💡 'Get the hang of it' is the natural idiom for mastering a skill; 'understand how to do' is incomplete and too literal.
8. ❌ *She made the entire meal from zero with just a pan and fresh ingredients.*
   ✅ **She made the entire meal from scratch with just a pan and fresh ingredients.**
   💡 'From scratch' is the standard English expression for starting without shortcuts; 'from zero' is a direct translation that English speakers don't use.
